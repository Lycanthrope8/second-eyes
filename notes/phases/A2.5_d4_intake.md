Status, kept as separate statuses:

1. **Functional implementation:** built and tested here; not yet run on the headset.
2. **Uncached numerical acceptance against float32:** not established (delivery 1).
3. **Cached numerical acceptance:** not established. The session is operational, so it runs path U only, and prefix

The design choices below are mine, inside D98 and D99. They are for ChatGPT's review.

## 1. D99 clause by clause

**(1) One request path for the ADB inbox and the panel's presets.**

- Both become a `Ticket` in one `SessionCore` and run through delivery 3's pipeline, as `ExecutionPurpose.Operational`
  with `CacheMode.Off`.
- There is no keyboard, ADB is development transport only, and all grounding stays on the headset.

**(2) The inbox protocol.**

- The PC pushes `files/interactive/inbox/(ID).json.tmp`, then renames it to `(ID).json` on the headset (`adb shell mv`).
  The headset reads only `*.json`, so it never sees a partial file.
- The request record is `a25_interactive_request`, format 1. It carries:
  - a request ID matching `^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.-]{0,63}$`;
  - `expected_scene`: the snapshot ID, scene ID, scene revision, and the SHA-256 of the snapshot file;
  - the command record, whose scene ID and revision must equal `expected_scene`'s;
  - `command_kind`: `dataset_command` or `written`.
- **Each ID is processed once per session.** It is claimed in a ledger before anything else. A later file with the
  same ID gets only `outbox/(ID).duplicate-(time).json`, and the first acknowledgement and result stand.
- **The answers go under the same ID:**
  - `outbox/(ID).ack.json`: accepted, or rejected with a reason;
  - `outbox/(ID).result.json`: the outcome with its stage times.
  - Both are written to a temporary file and renamed.
- **Rejected explicitly, as `refused` outcomes:**
  - `invalid_request`, a schema failure, with its reason;
  - `stale_scene_binding`, when the expected scene differs from the current one; the differing field is named;
  - `no_scene_bound`.
- **Unanswerable files.** A file without a usable request ID can't be answered under one. It is kept in
  `inbox/processed/` and logged as an event.
- Every processed file moves to `inbox/processed/`, as evidence.

**(3) Timing**, on one headset stopwatch:

- the stages: detected, validated, queued, inference start, inference end, outcome;
- `app_observed_ms` = outcome − detected;
- for presets, "detected" is the button event, and for the inbox it is the file's detection.

The PC's push-and-rename time is in the PC's receipt only, and is never combined with the headset's clock.

**D98: cancellation and stale-result rejection.**

- **Cancel** answers queued requests at once (`cancelled_while_queued`). A native evaluation in progress cannot be
  interrupted without a native change, so its finished result becomes `cancelled` (`cancelled_during_evaluation`).
  The target is discarded, and the earlier status is kept in the detail.
- **Switching the scene** starts a new epoch. A result for the previous epoch becomes `refused`
  (`stale_result_scene_changed`), its target discarded.
- **Neither rule touches a technical failure,** which keeps its own reason, and neither turns anything into an ASK.

**(5) Scenes and presets.**

- The scenes are the three annotated golden snapshots (3, 6 and 10 objects), read through the golden check's
  hash-checked reader.
- The presets are their golden dataset commands. Written fixtures are not presets: they are implementation checks only.

## 2. On the headset

The panel has a new row:

- **Session (A2.5 d4):** start or end. Starting loads the model once (8,192 tokens, 2 threads, flags 0) and starts the
  inbox and the worker.
- **Scene:** cycles through the three snapshots.
- **Preset:** sends the current scene's next dataset command.
- **Cancel.**

The text box lists the scene's objects with their letters (D104's serialized order, K last), marks the chosen one with
an arrow, and shows the last outcome and its app-observed time. The status line names the request, its outcome, its
time and its execution path.

Each session writes `files/interactive/sessions/(UTC time)/`: `session.json`, and `outcomes.jsonl` with every answered
request, presets included.

Its events are `interactive.session.start`, `interactive.scene`, `interactive.result`,
`interactive.inbox.unanswerable`, `interactive.inbox.error` and `interactive.worker.error`.

## 3. On the laptop

- `python -m grounding.quest inbox-send --run RUN --goldens DIR --snapshot 3|6|10` with one command source: `--dataset
  N`, `--command-file F` or `--text "..."`. Optional: `--request-id ID`, and `--resend` to deliver an ID again on
  purpose.
- `python -m grounding.quest outbox-pull --run RUN` brings the acknowledgements, results, duplicate notices and the newest
  session's log, with a summary per request.

## 4. Testing here

- **The intake core on .NET 8, at C# 9:** 15 checks, alongside delivery 3's 19 (34 in all).
  - validation, good and bad;
  - the scene binding check;
  - processed-once;
  - cancel while queued and during evaluation;
  - a stale result;
  - failures keeping their reasons;
  - the stage times;
  - answers without evaluation.
- **The service** type-checks with the runtime against the Unity stubs: 0 errors, 0 warnings.
- **The panel's session partial** type-checks against a shell of the panel members it uses.
- **The laptop's inbox,** on a labelled fake device with fixture goldens: 16 checks.
  - the request's binding and labels;
  - the temporary name and rename;
  - the receipts;
  - `--dataset` and `--resend`;
  - the refusals;
  - the pull, its summary and its read-only use of the headset.

## 5. What this does not establish

- Numerical acceptance of any kind.
- Speech, or Gate A.
- Latency acceptance, which is delivery 5's measurement.

A U evaluation takes about 17 seconds on the headset (r016), so a command's app-observed time is dominated by it.
