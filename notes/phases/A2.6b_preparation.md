## 1. What it does

`python -m grounding.preparation.iref_vla_dev` has two commands, one for each freeze (correction 2).

**`freeze`** fixes, before any preparation, one hashed specification (`spec.json`) holding:

- the official-first partition and every environment's assignment;
- the cap: 64 parents per environment;
- the eligibility rules;
- the sampling procedure and its salt (`second-eyes/a26/sampling/v1`);
- the desktop subset's rule (salt `second-eyes/a26/desktop/v1`; 32 parents × both views, `coordinates_v2`; 64
  requests);
- the analysis plan: a paired environment-cluster bootstrap, B = 10,000, the literal seed 20261010, 95% percentile
  intervals, weights recomputed in each replicate;
- the closed partitions.

**`prepare`** requires that specification and refuses an inventory whose hash differs from it. It then:

1. **Reads only the development and legacy scenes.** No other scene's zip member is read (`release.read_source(only=…)`).
2. **Runs the accepted chain per scene,** in parallel worker processes, with every scene accounted for, and failures
   recorded:
   - A2.2a's import, under the scene's release identity (`release.import_release_scene`);
   - A2.2c's selection audit;
   - A2.2d's materialization, all with `sample_only=False`.
3. **Resolves each parent's annotation entries** (correction 1).
4. **Applies eligibility.** A parent is eligible under A2.3a's accepted rule (both views materialized, all four
   view-format renderings succeeded), with 3 to 10 objects in both views, and a unique target. Every exclusion keeps its
   reason.
5. **Samples per environment** by the frozen procedure, recording N and n (correction 3).
6. **Builds four D104 requests per selected parent:** both views, both formats, path U, cache off.
7. **Hashes everything:**
   - the request manifest;
   - the sampling record;
   - the desktop subset;
   - review copies of the download receipt, the inventory summary and the partition proposal;
   - the reference-only answers, in their own folder.

## 2. The corrections from ChatGPT's review, as implemented

**1. Multiple entries.** My freeze proposal said all 3,744 multi-entry texts have non-unique targets. That was wrong:
the collector had counted entries, and never compared their targets (`failures.md`). Each parent's entries are now
resolved, independent of their order:

- **The target:** one target over all entries is `unique_target`, counted once, with its annotation IDs. Several
  targets are `conflicting_targets`, quarantined from unique-target scoring.
- **The parsing label:** relation, relation type and anchor sets are compared separately, and give `consistent` or
  `disagreeing`. A unique target is not a unique parsing label.
- **The stratum:** the relation when the entries agree on it; otherwise the explicit stratum `relation_disagreement`.
- Nothing is called semantic ambiguity.

**2. Two freezes.** The partition, cap and procedures are frozen now, and the request manifest after preparation. The
proposal's 2,789 parents and 47 environments were planning estimates. The run reports the final counts:

- eligible and selected parents;
- exclusion reasons;
- contributing environments;
- zero-eligible environments, which are reported, never replaced.

The assigned environments and the salts are fixed. A parent whose four requests do not all build is left out whole, so
the manifest stays matched across formats and views, and its failures are listed.

**3. Weighting.**

- **Sampling.** Strata are filled round-robin, in ascending stratum name, one parent per nonempty stratum per round, up
  to the cap. Within a stratum, parents are taken by ascending SHA-256(salt LF environment LF stratum LF parent).
- **The analysis plan** fixes two weightings:
  - command-weighted: weight N/n, estimating over the eligible development commands;
  - environment-weighted: each environment's eligible relation proportions restored, then equal environment weight.
- The unweighted sample is shown only with that label.

## 3. Changes to accepted code

**The one-scene converter** (`convert.py`, `output.py`) now takes an identity:

- `K` is a proxy over the identity in force;
- `PINNED_IDENTITY` holds A2.2a's exact constants and its four descriptive strings.

**Proof:**

- importing the pinned sample with the original and the refactored code gives 1,942 files, none differing by SHA-256;
- A2.2a's suite passes 96 of 96, and A2.2c's passes 83 of 83 on the real sample;
- A2.2d's 53 unit checks pass. Its real-sample stage had no failure before the sandbox's time limit.

**The A2.5 code is unchanged.** The D104 request record mirrors `prompt_goldens._golden`, with the format as a
parameter. The relations format takes A2.2d's relation work cap, from each bundle's own summary (1,190), as A2.2d and
A2.3d pass it.

## 4. Verification (`grounding/tests/test_iref_vla_dev.py`, 20 checks; Python 3.11 and 3.12)

**The fixture** is a small release built from the pinned scene, renamed to real ScanNet list names: development
(`scene0081_00` and `_01`, one environment; `scene0063_00`), calibration, training and the legacy scene.

- Each scene has its own object fingerprint. One unused object is lengthened, consistently in the CSV and the scene
  graph, including the graph's volume.
- `scene0063_00` carries correction 1's two cases, built from consistent existing entries.

**The checks:**

- the round-robin allocation and N and n, on synthetic rows, including a zero-eligible environment;
- correction 1, on synthetic and real records;
- the freeze's assignments and its hashed contents;
- the refusal of a changed inventory;
- that calibration and training scenes are never read from the zip;
- that every scene is prepared;
- the eligibility rule;
- the strata against the eligible rows;
- four matched requests per parent;
- **a `coordinates_v2` request equal to A2.5's D104 golden construction:** document, prompt, tokens, mapping and both
  boundaries;
- the desktop subset;
- answers absent from the requests;
- the manifest's hashes;
- the legacy labelling.

**The real command line** was also run on the fixture: `freeze`, then `prepare` with two worker processes started by
`spawn`, as on Windows. It gave 4 scenes prepared and 88 requests with no failure. The same inputs prepared in one
process give identical request, sampling, desktop and specification hashes.
