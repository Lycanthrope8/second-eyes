## 2. Final configuration

The left passthrough camera at its default 1280x960. After the permission is granted, `GetSupportedResolutions`
lists eleven sizes from 320x240 to 1280x1280; before it, the list is empty. Frames are told apart by MRUK's
`IsUpdatedThisFrame` (frame signal `updated_flag`). Preview off; camera off at launch.

The installed app requests `horizonos.permission.HEADSET_CAMERA` (ours; granted when the prompt was accepted) and
also `USE_SCENE` (never granted) and `USE_ANCHOR_API` (granted at install), each in its `com.oculus` and `horizonos`
forms. Those last two come from MRUK's build, not from our manifest (O25).

### Profiling session, run `20261006_A1_r044` (6 October 2026)

- The diagnostic ran at 8 steps per frame, not 4: the scene kept the steps list saved by the earlier build
  (`notes/failures.md`). One scan at 0.5 Hz: p95 529 ms; stale 0.74% over the scan, 0.88% over the hover blocks, and
  0.69 to 1.02% over the buckets overlapping inference across the clock range (judged at 1.02%); 1.22 stale frames per
  keyframe (1.44 at 1 Hz in r043). The 4-step test was not repeated; the profiling passes, one step at a time, ask the
  same question more sharply.
- Clock: the eight sync stalls fixed the offset at -0.70 s, equally good from -0.75 to -0.65 s (r = 0.72), narrower
  than r043's fit from incidental misses.
- FrameTimingManager gave no per-frame GPU times, so step costs could not be measured. D89's branch for this case is
  shape-based estimates, labelled as estimates and validated on the headset.
- The five profiling passes ran (280 single steps, four empty frames apart). `analysis/detector_phases.py` now places
  the stale frames during them by step: whether single steps miss on their own, and roughly which.
- CPU parity (r042) confirmed: strict agreement on all five canvases. The tool's earlier "PARITY FAILED" came from
  demanding GPU results in a CPU run; it now gives a verdict per backend.
- First estimate (from the ONNX graph with a hypothesized layer list; the real list is in r044's log): the stem
  convolution after the Focus slicing is the single heaviest step, about a tenth of an inference. If that one step
  alone exceeds the frame's spare GPU time, no schedule can remove one miss per keyframe, and D89's rule says to report
  it rather than overload a frame.
