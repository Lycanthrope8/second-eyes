# A1.10 · Object detector on passthrough

Status: A1.10a (camera access and its cost) is complete. A1.10b to A1.10e are proposals awaiting approval.

## 1. What was built (A1.10a)

- `quest-app/Assets/SecondEyes/Perception/IFrameSource.cs`: the frame-source interface every detector reads, so the
  same detector later serves A4's recorded-video replay and A6's drone stream.
- `quest-app/Assets/SecondEyes/Perception/PassthroughFrameSource.cs`: the passthrough camera through MRUK's
  `PassthroughCameraAccess`; it requests only the headset-camera permission, starts the camera only when asked and
  logs its state about once per second.
- `quest-app/Assets/SecondEyes/App/CameraButton.cs`: X on the left controller turns the camera on or off.
- `quest-app/Assets/Plugins/Android/AndroidManifest.xml`: `horizonos.permission.HEADSET_CAMERA`.
- `analysis/camera_frames.py`, which reads a run's session logs; `schemas/log-event.v1.json` and `docs/logging.md`
  (six `camera.*` events); `docs/profiling.md` (the procedure); `docs/meta-ai.md` (the camera facts).
- In Unity, by the project lead: MRUK `com.meta.xr.mrutilitykit` 207.0.0, and in `SampleScene` a `PassthroughCamera`
  object holding MRUK's Passthrough Camera Access (left camera, default resolution, disabled at launch),
  `PassthroughFrameSource` and `CameraButton`.

Before delivery, the three scripts compiled with Mono's compiler, warnings as errors, against stand-in types, with
`CurrentResolution` as `Vector2Int` and as `Vector2`; the Unity build was the real check, and it compiled.

## 2. Final configuration

The left passthrough camera at its default 1280x960. After the permission is granted, `GetSupportedResolutions`
lists eleven sizes from 320x240 to 1280x1280; before it, the list is empty. Frames are told apart by MRUK's
`IsUpdatedThisFrame` (frame signal `updated_flag`). Preview off; camera off at launch.

The installed app requests `horizonos.permission.HEADSET_CAMERA` (ours; granted when the prompt was accepted) and
also `USE_SCENE` (never granted) and `USE_ANCHOR_API` (granted at install), each in its `com.oculus` and `horizonos`
forms. Those last two come from MRUK's build, not from our manifest (O25).

Over run B's 693 full playing seconds the camera delivered a new frame in every second; its lowest full seconds were
48.4 frames/s at the moment of quitting (703 s, after the recording), 50.9 at 283 s and 52.0 at 35 s (before the
recording). `analysis/profile.py compare` found frame rate and memory within the repeat-run limits, and CPU
(+7.3 points against 3) and app GPU time (+6% against 5%) outside them. Those limits are for repeat runs of one
condition, so "outside" here marks the camera's measurable cost: clearly for CPU, only just for GPU time.

Caveats: one run per condition, as in A1; run B followed run A and started about 4 °C warmer, and its GPU level
reached 3. Read the GPU-time difference as about half a millisecond, not precisely.

Interpretation: at its default setting the camera alone keeps the display at 72 Hz with no stale frames and no
memory increase, at a cost of about 7 points of headset CPU, half a millisecond of GPU time per frame and 0.4 W.
CPU is the resource the detector and llama.cpp (two threads on cores 3 to 5, D59) will compete for.
